Travel and Reimbursement Policy

A quick guide to ARC’s Expense Authorization, Travel and Reimbursement Policy: when you need approval, who signs, what ARC will pay for, and the deadlines that decide whether you get reimbursed. Effective May 1, 2018, revised August 1, 2024.

The policy in thirteen points

  • Overnight or out-of-state travel needs an approved Expense Authorization (EA) in Deltek Costpoint before anything is booked. An EA received after the travel date means no reimbursement.
  • Expense Reports (ER) are due to Finance within 40 days of trip end or expense date. At 60 days the claim is not reimbursed; any special-exception payment after 60 days is taxable.
  • Actual costs more than 10% over the EA need the ARC Director’s signature.
  • Meals are paid as GSA per diem, not actual cost: 75% on first and last days, minus any meals provided.
  • Book the lowest logical airfare from refundable, non-penalty tickets. Travelers who pick a nonrefundable or penalty fare pay any change costs themselves, unless an extraordinary business need forces the change. Don’t choose an airline for points or perks when it isn’t the lowest logical fare.
  • Fly economy. Business and first class are not paid on domestic flights except to Alaska and Hawaii; international business class needs advance approval. Federally funded international flights must use U.S. carriers or code-shares. Upgrades are at the traveler’s own expense.
  • Least expensive lodging and most cost-effective ground transport. No upgrades at ARC’s expense.
  • Out-of-state travel is by air unless another method is documented as more cost-efficient or practical and approved. A personal car is appropriate only when other options are exhausted, unreasonable or not cost-effective.
  • Request policy exceptions before booking. Detail the exception and its justification in the EA; the CFO gives final approval. After-the-fact requests are reviewed closely and may be denied.
  • Estimate mileage at the current IRS rate, which can change each year, minus normal commuting miles. The rate covers gas and wear; tolls and reasonable parking are paid separately.
  • Travel goes on reimbursement, not the P-Card, except conference registration.
  • Grant rules win when they are more restrictive than this policy.
  • No one approves their own EA or ER. Repeated noncompliance escalates from verbal warning to a corrective action plan.

      Air travel

      • Lowest logical airfare from refundable, non-penalty fares.
      • No business or first class domestically, except Alaska and Hawaii. International business class with advance approval.
      • Fly America Act: federally funded international trips use U.S. carriers or code-shares.
      • First checked bag reimbursed; extra or overweight bags need a business reason.
      • Not paid: priority seating, upgrades, airline clubs.
      • Cancel at least 1 hour before departure; use credits within a year, for ARC business only.

      Vehicles

      • Fleet: book on the intranet Vehicle Booking Calendar with a real business purpose in the Title field.
      • Return with at least half a tank, by 6:30 AM the next business day if kept overnight.
      • Enterprise account 03HARC1: in-state rentals include coverage, so decline extra insurance; opt in outside Georgia.
      • Rentals over 24 hours need prior written authorization, copied to General Services.
      • Personal car: IRS rate, minus normal commute; tolls and reasonable parking extra.
      • Never reimbursed: tickets, fines or moving violations in any vehicle.

      Lodging

      • Outside the region only; in-region lodging needs written CFO pre-approval.
      • Ask for government or conference rate; at a conference hotel, the conference rate is the limit.
      • Georgia hotels: submit the Hotel Occupancy Tax Exemption Form at check-in. The $5 per night state fee still applies.
      • Avoid nonrefundable rates unless conference lodging requires them; no-shows aren’t paid.
      • Resort fees are reimbursable; paid upgrades are not.
      • Shared room: the person who paid claims the full cost.

      Employee group meals

      • Only when staff must stay on site: emergencies or mandatory meetings with 4+ hours of agenda and no meal break.
      • Written approval by Department Manager or higher before the event.
      • Invoice preferred; P-Card allowed.
      • Not for lunch meetings where the meal is the meeting.
      • Document: itemized receipt with tip, purpose, attendee and RSVP lists.

      ARC-hosted events

      • Food for mostly non-employee audiences (Board, committees) follows procurement rules and funder allowability.
      • Don’t buy food just for staff.
      • Invoice preferred; P-Card allowed.
      • Document: itemized receipt with tip, purpose, attendee and RSVP lists.

      Executive-hosted meals

      • Executive leadership and approved employees only, for advisors or visiting dignitaries.
      • Not for routine visits by funders, jurisdictions or consultants.
      • Reimbursement only, never the P-Card; no alcohol.
      • Prior ED/CEO approval and a budgeted line item.

      P-Card and travel

      • Travel on the P-Card (other than registration) means escalating discipline and repayment by the cardholder.
      • The cost can then be claimed through an ER if allowable.
      • P-Card purchases without an itemized receipt are repaid by the employee.

      Noncompliance and exceptions

      Findings are counted per policy and reset two years after the first notice. Three or more across policies escalates discipline.

      1st findingVerbal warning from the direct supervisor, followed by written documentation
      2nd findingWritten warning to the employee file; supervisor, Department Manager and Executive notified
      3rd findingWritten warning in the personnel file and a Corrective Action Plan with the CFO and Talent Management

      Corrective Action Plan includes

      • The problem and its root cause
      • Who is accountable
      • Simple, measurable solutions with an owner each
      • Achievable deadlines
      • Progress monitoring

      Requesting an exception

      • With an EA: detail it in the EA and route normally; CFO gives final approval.
      • Without an EA: written request from the cognizant Executive to the CFO before travel, with names, purpose, exception, justification, expense types and dates.
      • Exceptions cover only the named event and dates; after-the-fact and repeat requests are likely denied.

      Accommodations

      • Department Heads can approve reasonable accommodations for travelers with disabilities, even at higher cost.
      • The approver documents and files the extra costs.

      Items worth clarifying in the next revision

      • Day 60 is ambiguous. General Provisions bar reimbursement for ERs received “after 60 days”; Section 6.3 bars them at “60 days or more.” The calculators use the stricter reading.
      • EA scope for day trips: the Introduction and General Provisions require an EA only for overnight or out-of-state travel, but the employee responsibilities section says all out-of-region expenditures “should” be approved in advance with an EA, which would reach in-state day trips outside the region.
      • “ARC Director’s signature” for costs more than 10% over the EA is not a defined role.
      • Tuesday cutoff exceptions go to the CFO in General Provisions but to the Finance Department in Section 6.3.
      • Advances: up to 3 outstanding advances are allowed, but Section 6.2 also says any outstanding advance must be settled before another is issued.
      • Broken cross-references: Sections 6.5 (receipts, now 6.6), 7.5 and 7.10 are cited but don’t exist; the incidentals definition says “See for further definition.” Sections 4.5, 4.6 and 4.8 and Section Three’s heading are missing from the table of contents.
      • Missing recipient: executive-hosted meal reimbursements “shall be communicated to” no one named. The Primary Workstation definition still refers to a “State employee.”
      • Policy names don’t match the Payment Policy: this document cites the “Purchasing and Procurement Policy” and “Purchasing and Procurement Guide.”
